03 · quantization and ebedded

Does the smaller actor keep useful control?

I compare one FP32 actor with its W8A8HI Rust version in 2560 paired ground target episodes. Each pair starts from matched conditions and runs for 90 simulated seconds at 20 Hz. The two controllers then develop their own trajectories.

The wheel test removes torque from one randomly selected wheel at 20 s. The tracker challenge starts without tracker lock and uses a 10% packet loss setting.

Mean tracking duty during 30–90 s
Condition FP32 tracking duty Rust tracking duty
Ground tracking 79.16% 78.98%
Ground tracking wheel failure 6.27% 7.12%
Ground tracking 84.26% 84.22%

Tracking duty is the fraction of samples with angle ≤ 1°, reported rate norm ≤ 0.01 rad/s and a valid reference.

All results remain close after quantization. The wheel failure case is more demanding: both versions have low tracking duty after the fault, even though many episodes settled earlier. This points to a shared control difficulty rather than a large overall change caused by Rust inference.

Check the arithmetic separately

Before comparing trajectories, I feed identical observations to the implementations. This separates the effect of quantization from a disagreement between Numpy and Rust.

2_764_800 observations
Same input comparison Mean maximum action error Largest action error
Numpy W8A8HI vs FP32 0.012443 0.061323
Rust W8A8HI vs Numpy W8A8HI 0.00000106 0.014353

Errors use normalized action units. For each observation, take the largest difference across its four outputs, then summarize those values. Across all five runs, Rust vs. Numpy differences exceeded 10−4 threshold on 1848 of 4_608_000 observations (0.0401%). Most outputs agree closely, with occasional larger differences.

The actor has 19200 weights and 260 biases. Its linear layers require 19200 MACs per inference: 0.384 MMAC/s at 20 Hz, plus bias additions and nonlinearities.

In the three nominal host runs, the median Python to Rust request took about 81 μs and the 99th percentile about 119–120 μs. Occasional maxima reached 103–109 ms. This timing includes JSON communication and scheduling, the simulator waited for each reply.
