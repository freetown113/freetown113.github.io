01ENVIRONMENT

Learn actuator decisions inside a GNC loop

The agent controls the orientation of a rigid body through four reaction wheels. Guidance abstraction provides a target attitude and angular rate. A state estimator and fault-management path supply the controller’s belief about body's position and attitude. An RL agent learns to turn that information into motor commands.

The simulator is implemented in phases, each time another abstraction is added: base satellite physics; envoronment vectorization; wheels faulure modes; sensors measurements replace ground truth from physics; signal/device degradation or complete loss; supervisor modes; mission scenarios; orbital configuration. The physics at 100 Hz, sensors measurements at range from 80 to 100Hz, executes control at 20 Hz, with 512 parallel environments configured for training and evaluation.

Partial observability is deliberate: delayed measurements, estimated gyro bias, and inferred actuator authority replace direct access to the simulator’s state. The current agent is feedforward. Previous actions and explicit estimates provide context; there is no recurrent memory in the published network shape.

02TRAINING HISTORY

Imitation learning: PD controller as a teacher

During the training process, I gradually changed what the algorithm was responsible for controlling, fundamentally changing the neural network’s action space across three distinct phases. The first phase used residual PD control, where the agent learned a small correction on top of the PD controller. It then moved to direct body-torque control, where the agent had to determine the total torque required around the three axes of the satellite body. Finally, I moved to direct wheel-motor control, where the agent directly commands the individual control inputs of the four reaction-wheel motors. Each transition changes both the action space and the level of responsibility given to the agent.

A useful practical step was supervised initialization using the PD controller as a teacher. Training an RL policy entirely from scratch is difficult at the beginning because an untrained neural network produces essentially random actions, which can lead to unstable behavior and slow learning. Imitation learning helps to bypass this initial problem. I collected trajectories from untrained, partially trained, and trained residual policies and paired their observations with actions produced by the teacher. The policy mean was passed through tanh and trained using mean-squared error against the teacher actions.

L_imitation = mean(tanh(μθ(o)) − A_teacher)²

This gave the actor–critic network a useful baseline behavior before reinforcement learning started. Instead of beginning with completely random control, the agent already had a reasonable ability to stabilize the satellite and could explore useful states rather than spending most of its early training on unstable trajectories.

This procedure initialized the actor–critic network before further RL experiments with direct control. Its main benefit was providing useful initial behavior and better state-space coverage. However, imitation learning does not guarantee that learned behavior can be transferred directly between different action spaces. For example, a three-component body-torque output cannot simply be copied into a four-component reaction-wheel output. An explicit mapping between body torque and individual wheel commands is required. Imitation learning can provide a strong starting point, but it does not automatically bridge the structural difference when the action space changes.

03OPTIMIZATION

Algorithm details

The agent was trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). PPO uses collected rollouts together with a clipped policy objective to prevent excessively large policy updates. The new policy is optimized relative to the policy that generated the rollouts, while the clipping mechanism limits how far the update can move from that behavior. Given an observation, the actor produces the mean values of the action distribution. During training, actions are sampled from a normal distribution parameterized by the actor output, providing the exploration required for learning. A separate critic estimates the value function, which is used to calculate advantage estimates. Together, these components provide the optimization mechanism used to update the neural-network parameters.

25inputs→128 × 128tanh hidden layers→4motor commands

The actor and critic are separate networks, each containing 20,356 parameters. Keeping these parameter counts separate is important when estimating inference cost, since only the actor is required for policy inference. The current configuration uses a fixed policy log standard deviation of −1.5.

The configured success condition combines an attitude error below 1°, a rate error below 0.01 rad/s, and a 1-second dwell time. This is an evaluation criterion rather than a direct reconstruction of the reward function.

The reward itself is a more complex function composed of several terms, including attitude_error, attitude_error_progress, rate, action_smoothness, wheel_speed, target_success, and other components.

Algorithm background: Schulman et al., Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms.

04CURRICULUM / SCENARIOS

Increase the difficulty in steps

I do not ask the agent to solve every difficult condition from its first rollout. A curriculum changes the reset distribution and task conditions as training progresses: larger initial attitude errors, more demanding motion, imperfect measurements and faults.

A cone is a special reset mode, where the initial orientation is sampled within a configured angular bound around a reference orientation. The only restriction on the initial attitude error that the angle can vary inside that bound.

Moving targets add another difficulty. For ground tracking, a pass-centered reset gives the agent useful visible pass experience, that does not guarantee that the target remains visible for the whole episode. Mission phases must still manage acquisition, tracking and leaving the pass.

As more estimation and fault logic enter the loop, an episode takes more computation, and learning can also require more episodes. Faster simulation and an easier curriculum solve different parts of this problem.

05GENERALIZATION

Look beyond one successful episode

I check pointing, rate error, settling, wheel speeds and command behavior. With faults, I also look at what the monitor estimated and how the supervisor reacted. A short moment inside the tolerance is different from keeping the target there. The aim of the project is to obtain a compact policy that adapts to varios conditions proposed in scenarios.
